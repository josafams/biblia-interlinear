2 Corinthians Πρὸς Κορινθίους Βʹ

2 Corinthians is the most personal of Paul's letters. After 1 Corinthians, the relationship with the church deteriorated: there was a painful visit, an offense against Paul and a letter written “with many tears” (2:4). In chapters 1 to 7, relieved by the good news brought by Titus, Paul explains the changes in his plans and reflects on the ministry of the new covenant, carried by frail people (“treasure in jars of clay,” 4:7) and devoted to reconciliation with God. Chapters 8 and 9 ask the Corinthians to complete the collection for the poor in Jerusalem. In chapters 10 to 13, the tone changes: Paul confronts rival preachers, whom he ironically calls “super-apostles” (12:11), and defends himself by speaking not of successes but of sufferings and weaknesses.

Structure

  1. Comfort and change of plans chs. 1–2

    Paul gives thanks for God's comfort in the danger he went through in Asia, explains why he did not return to Corinth as he had promised and asks forgiveness for the one who offended him.

  2. Paul's ministry chs. 2–7

    The new covenant, of the Spirit, compared with that of Moses; the message carried in frail bodies; hope in the face of death; the appeal for reconciliation; and the joy at the news from Titus (2:14–7:16).

  3. The collection for Jerusalem chs. 8–9

    The example of the poor churches of Macedonia, the sending of Titus and other brothers to Corinth and the invitation to give generously and cheerfully.

  4. Defense against the rivals chs. 10–13

    Paul answers those who found him strong in his letters and weak in person, speaks “as a fool” of his own credentials and sufferings and announces the third visit.
