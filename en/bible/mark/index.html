Mark Κατὰ Μᾶρκον

Mark is the shortest of the gospels and tells the story of Jesus at a fast pace, marked by the repetition of “immediately” (in Greek, euthys). It does not narrate the birth: it begins with the preaching of John the Baptist and the baptism of Jesus and follows His ministry in Galilee, with healings, exorcisms, parables and conflicts with the religious leaders. At the center of the book, Peter recognizes Jesus as the Christ (8:29), and from then on Jesus announces three times that He will be handed over, killed and raised, while the disciples struggle to understand. The last week in Jerusalem takes up more than a third of the text (chs. 11–16). In the oldest manuscripts, the book ends at 16:8, with the women fleeing the empty tomb, seized with fear.

Structure

  1. Beginning in Galilee chs. 1–3

    John the Baptist, the baptism and the temptation; the call of the first disciples, healings in Capernaum, the first conflicts with the Pharisees and the choice of the Twelve.

  2. Parables and miracles chs. 4–5

    Parables about the seed and the Kingdom, the calming of the storm, the demon-possessed man of Gerasa, the woman who touches Jesus' cloak and Jairus's daughter.

  3. Mission and misunderstanding chs. 6–8

    The rejection at Nazareth, the sending of the Twelve, the death of John the Baptist, two feedings of the crowds and journeys to regions of Gentile majority, while the disciples are slow to understand (to 8:26).

  4. On the way chs. 8–10

    Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi opens the central part: three announcements of the passion, the transfiguration and teachings on serving, up to the healing of blind Bartimaeus at Jericho.

  5. In Jerusalem chs. 11–13

    The entry into the city, the fruitless fig tree, the cleansing of the temple, the debates with the leaders and the discourse on the destruction of the temple and the end.

  6. Passion and empty tomb chs. 14–16

    The anointing at Bethany, the Last Supper, the arrest, the trials, the crucifixion, the burial and the announcement of the resurrection to the women; then follows the longer ending (16:9–20).
