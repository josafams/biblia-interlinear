Philemon Πρὸς Φιλήμονα

Philemon is the shortest of Paul's letters, a note of a single chapter. In prison, Paul writes to Philemon, a Christian in whose house a church meets, about Onesimus, his slave, who became a Christian while with Paul. Without giving orders, although he says he could, Paul asks Philemon to receive him “no longer as a slave” but “as a beloved brother” (1:16), offers to pay for any loss and hints that he expects even more than he asks. The letter is a living example of the art of persuasion in antiquity and an important text in discussions about the Christian faith and slavery.

Structure

  1. Greeting and gratitude ch. 1

    Paul and Timothy greet Philemon, Apphia, Archippus and the church in the house; Paul gives thanks for Philemon's love and faith, which has refreshed the saints (1:1–7).

  2. The request for Onesimus ch. 1

    Paul appeals instead of ordering; Onesimus, formerly “useless,” is now useful; may Philemon receive him as he would receive Paul himself, who takes on any debt (1:8–21).

  3. Plans and greetings ch. 1

    Paul asks them to prepare a guest room for him, since he hopes to be released, and sends greetings from Epaphras, Mark, Aristarchus, Demas and Luke (1:22–25).

Key verses

So although in Christ I am bold enough to order you to do what is proper, I prefer to appeal on the basis of love. For I, Paul, am now aged, and a prisoner of Christ Jesus as well.

I appeal to you for my child Onesimus, whose father I became while I was in chains. Formerly he was useless to you, but now he has become useful both to you and to me.

Philemon 1:10–11

For perhaps this is why he was separated from you for a while, so that you might have him back for good— no longer as a slave, but better than a slave, as a beloved brother. He is especially beloved to me, but even more so to you, both in person and in the Lord.
