Leviticus וַיִּקְרָא

Leviticus gathers the instructions Moses receives in the newly erected tabernacle about how Israel can live close to a holy God. Chapters 1 to 7 describe the sacrifices and offerings; chapters 8 to 10 tell of the ordination of Aaron and his sons and the death of Nadab and Abihu. Then come the laws on clean and unclean (food, childbirth, skin disease, bodily discharges) and the annual ritual of the Day of Atonement (ch. 16). Chapters 17 to 26 extend the demand for holiness to daily life: family, sexuality, work, trade, the calendar of feasts and the possession of the land, with the sabbatical year and the Jubilee. There is almost no narrative, and the whole book takes place at the foot of Mount Sinai.

Structure

  1. Sacrifices and offerings chs. 1–7

    The main kinds of offering (burnt offering, grain offering, peace offering, sin offering and guilt offering) and the instructions to the priests about each one.

  2. The beginning of the priesthood chs. 8–10

    Moses consecrates Aaron and his sons; Aaron offers the first sacrifices, and Nadab and Abihu die when they present unauthorized fire.

  3. Clean and unclean chs. 11–15

    Animals that may or may not be eaten, purification after childbirth, skin disease and mildew in clothing and houses, and bodily discharges.

  4. The Day of Atonement ch. 16

    The annual rite in which Aaron enters behind the veil, before the ark, and a goat is sent into the wilderness carrying the people's iniquities.

  5. Laws of holiness chs. 17–26

    Blood and the place of sacrifice, forbidden relations, everyday ethics, rules for the priests, the feasts, the sabbatical year and the Jubilee, and the blessings and punishments tied to the covenant.

  6. Vows and dedications ch. 27

    An appendix on the value of persons, animals and lands vowed to the LORD and on the redemption of tithes.
