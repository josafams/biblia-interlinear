Micah מִיכָה

Micah, a prophet from the countryside of Judah, announces judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem because of the greed of the powerful, who seize fields and houses, and the corruption of leaders, priests and paid prophets. Alternating threats and promises, the book announces the ruin of Zion, but also a time when the nations will go up to the mountain of the LORD and will no longer train for war, and a ruler who will come forth from Bethlehem. The book sums up what God asks: to act justly, to love mercy and to walk humbly with God (6:8).

Structure

  1. Judgment on the two kingdoms chs. 1–3

    Samaria and Jerusalem are condemned; denunciation of those who steal land and of leaders, prophets and priests who act for money.

  2. Hope for Zion chs. 4–5

    The mountain of the LORD above the others, peace among the nations, the remnant gathered and the ruler coming from Bethlehem.

  3. The lawsuit and trust chs. 6–7

    God brings His people to trial and reminds them of what is good; the book ends with lament, trust and praise to the God who forgives.

Chapters

  1. 1 Judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem
  2. 2 Against those who covet fields and houses
  3. 3 Against leaders, prophets and priests
  4. 4 The mountain of the LORD in the last days

Micah 4:3

But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are small among the clans of Judah, out of you will come forth for Me One to be ruler over Israel— One whose origins are of old, from the days of eternity.

He has shown you, O man, what is good. And what does the LORD require of you but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?

Who is a God like You, who pardons iniquity and passes over the transgression of the remnant of His inheritance— who does not retain His anger forever, because He delights in loving devotion?
