John Κατὰ Ἰωάννην

John tells the story of Jesus in a way quite different from the other three gospels. It opens with a poetic prologue about the Word (in Greek, Logos), who was with God from the beginning and “became flesh” (1:14). The first half of the book is organized around signs, such as water turned into wine at Cana and the raising of Lazarus, followed by long dialogues and discourses in which Jesus speaks of Himself with images introduced by “I am.” The second half concentrates on the last night with the disciples, with the foot washing and the farewell discourses (chs. 13–17), and then on the passion and the resurrection. The book itself states its purpose: that its readers may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in His name (20:31).

Structure

  1. Prologue and first disciples ch. 1

    The hymn to the Word (1:1–18), the testimony of John the Baptist, who points to Jesus as the Lamb of God, and the call of Andrew, Peter, Philip and Nathanael.

  2. Signs and discourses chs. 2–12

    Signs and dialogues in Cana, Jerusalem, Samaria and Galilee, across several Jewish feasts; opposition grows until the raising of Lazarus and the decision to kill Jesus, and the part ends with the announcement: “The hour has come” (12:23).

  3. The farewell chs. 13–17

    At the Last Supper, Jesus washes the disciples' feet, announces the betrayal and the denial, gives the commandment of love, promises the Advocate and prays for His own (ch. 17).

  4. Passion and death chs. 18–19

    The arrest in the garden beyond the Kidron, the hearings before Annas and Pilate, the crucifixion and the burial by Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus.

  5. Resurrection ch. 20

    Mary Magdalene at the tomb, the appearances to the disciples and to Thomas and the book's first conclusion (20:30–31).

  6. Epilogue ch. 21

    The appearance by the Sea of Tiberias, Jesus' dialogue with Peter and the final note about the beloved disciple and his testimony.
