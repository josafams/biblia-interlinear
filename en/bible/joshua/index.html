Joshua יְהוֹשֻׁעַ

Joshua tells how Israel, after the death of Moses, crosses the Jordan River and settles in Canaan, the land promised to the patriarchs. The first half of the book narrates the crossing, the capture of Jericho and Ai, the treaty with the Gibeonites and two great military campaigns, one in the south and the other in the north of the region. The second half describes the division of the land among the tribes, with lists of borders and cities, and sets apart the cities of refuge and the cities assigned to the Levites. The book ends with Joshua's farewell speeches and the renewal of the covenant at Shechem, where the people commit themselves to serve the LORD alone.

Structure

  1. Entering the land chs. 1–5

    Joshua takes command, the spies are taken in by Rahab in Jericho, the people cross the Jordan on dry ground and, camped at Gilgal, practice circumcision and celebrate the Passover.

  2. The conquests chs. 6–12

    The fall of Jericho, the failure and then the victory at Ai, the treaty with Gibeon, the campaigns in the south and the north and the list of the 31 defeated kings.

  3. Dividing the land chs. 13–21

    The portions of the tribes east and west of the Jordan, Caleb's inheritance, the assembly at Shiloh, the six cities of refuge and the 48 cities of the Levites.

  4. Farewells and covenant chs. 22–24

    The eastern tribes return home and build an altar that almost causes a war; Joshua takes leave of the leaders and renews the covenant at Shechem before he dies.
