Esther אֶסְתֵּר

At the court of the Persian king Xerxes (Ahasuerus in Hebrew), in Susa, the young Jewish woman Esther becomes queen without revealing her origin. When Haman, the most powerful official in the kingdom, obtains a decree to exterminate the Jews of the empire, Mordecai, Esther's cousin and guardian, persuades her to risk her life and intercede with the king. In a sequence of banquets and reversals, Haman is unmasked and executed, and a new decree allows the Jews to defend themselves. The book ends by explaining the origin of the feast of Purim.

Structure

  1. Esther comes to the throne chs. 1–2

    Queen Vashti is deposed, Esther is chosen, and Mordecai uncovers a conspiracy against the king.

  2. The threat chs. 3–4

    Mordecai does not bow before Haman, who obtains a decree against all the Jews; Esther decides to go to the king.

  3. The reversal chs. 5–7

    Esther's banquets, the king's sleepless night, the honoring of Mordecai and Haman's fall.

  4. Deliverance and Purim chs. 8–10

    The counter-decree, the Jews' defense, the institution of the feast and the greatness of Mordecai.
