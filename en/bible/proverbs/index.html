Proverbs מִשְׁלֵי

Proverbs is a collection of teachings on how to live wisely in everyday life: in the family, at work, in the use of words, in dealing with money, friends and authorities. Chapters 1 to 9 gather longer speeches, in which a father instructs his son and Wisdom herself, portrayed as a woman, calls to people in the streets and squares. From chapter 10 on, short sayings predominate, almost always of two lines, that contrast the wise and the fool, the righteous and the wicked, the hard worker and the sluggard. What unites the whole is the idea that the fear of the LORD is the starting point of knowledge and wisdom (1:7; 9:10). The book ends with the words of Agur and Lemuel and with a poem in praise of the wife of noble character.

Structure

  1. Prologue and instructions to the son chs. 1–9

    The purpose of the book (1:1–7), a father's advice to his son and the speeches of personified Wisdom. The part ends with two rival invitations: that of Wisdom and that of the woman Folly (ch. 9).

  2. Proverbs of Solomon chs. 10–22

    The first great collection (10:1–22:16), with 375 verses, almost all independent sayings of two lines.

  3. Sayings of the wise chs. 22–24

    Two smaller collections (22:17–24:22 and 24:23–34), with longer counsels, partly similar to an Egyptian wisdom work.

  4. Collection of the men of Hezekiah chs. 25–29

    Proverbs of Solomon “copied by the men of Hezekiah king of Judah” (25:1), with many comparisons drawn from nature and daily life.

  5. Words of Agur ch. 30

    Reflections on the limits of human knowledge, a short prayer and numerical sayings of the “three things… and four” kind.

  6. Lemuel and the wife of noble character ch. 31

    The counsel King Lemuel's mother taught him (31:1–9) and the alphabetic poem of the wife of noble character (31:10–31).
