1 Thessalonians Πρὸς Θεσσαλονικεῖς Αʹ

1 Thessalonians is probably Paul's oldest surviving letter and, for that reason, one of the first Christian writings. Paul, Silvanus and Timothy had been forced to leave Thessalonica shortly after founding there a church made up mainly of people who had turned away from idols (1:9). On receiving from Timothy good news about their steadfastness, Paul writes with relief, recalls the time he spent in the city and reinforces the teaching they received about holy living and work. He also answers a question that was grieving them: what happens to the brothers who die before the coming of the Lord (4:13–18).

Structure

  1. Gratitude for the church ch. 1

    The faith, love and hope of the Thessalonians have become known in Macedonia and Achaia.

  2. Memories of the mission chs. 2–3

    Paul recalls how he worked with his own hands so as not to be a burden to anyone, the church's suffering, the forced separation, the sending of Timothy from Athens and the joy at the news he brought.

  3. Instructions chs. 4–5

    Holiness in the body, brotherly love and work; hope regarding those who have died; the day of the Lord, which will come by surprise; and a series of brief counsels for the life of the church.

Chapters

  1. 1 The faith, love and hope of the Thessalonians
  2. 2 Paul's work in Thessalonica
  3. 3 Timothy brings good news
  4. 4 A holy life and those who have died
  5. 5 The day of the Lord; final exhortations
