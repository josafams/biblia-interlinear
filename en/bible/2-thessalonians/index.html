2 Thessalonians Πρὸς Θεσσαλονικεῖς Βʹ

2 Thessalonians is a short letter, with the same heading as 1 Thessalonians, addressed to a church that was still suffering persecution. The first chapter encourages it with the certainty that God will do justice when the Lord Jesus is revealed. The second corrects an idea that was frightening the readers, that the day of the Lord had already come, perhaps supported by a letter attributed to the missionaries themselves (2:2): before that day, the text says, the rebellion and the “man of lawlessness” will come, now held back by something or someone who “restrains” him. The last chapter deals with those who had stopped working and were living “idly.”

Structure

  1. Comfort in persecution ch. 1

    Thanksgiving for the faith that grows amid afflictions and the announcement of God's just judgment when the Lord Jesus appears with His angels.

  2. The day has not yet come ch. 2

    Before the day the rebellion and the “man of lawlessness,” who sets himself up in the temple of God, will come; for now, something restrains him. The readers must hold to the traditions received by word or by letter.

  3. Work and discipline ch. 3

    A request for prayer, an order that the idle should work and that those who do not obey be avoided, and a greeting written in Paul's own hand, presented as a mark in every letter (3:17).

Chapters

  1. 1 Perseverance in persecution and just judgment
  2. 2 The man of lawlessness before the day
  3. 3 Against idleness; a greeting in Paul's own hand

Key verses

Now concerning the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our being gathered together to Him, we ask you, brothers, not to be easily disconcerted or alarmed by any spirit or message or letter seeming to be from us, alleging that the Day of the Lord has already come. Let no one deceive you in any way, for it will not come until the rebellion occurs and the man of lawlessness—the son of destruction—is revealed.

2 Thessalonians 2:1–3

Therefore, brothers, stand firm and cling to the traditions we taught you, whether by speech or by letter.

2 Thessalonians 2:15

For even while we were with you, we gave you this command: “If anyone is unwilling to work, he shall not eat.”
