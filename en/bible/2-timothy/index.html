2 Timothy Πρὸς Τιμόθεον Βʹ

2 Timothy has the form of a farewell letter. Paul appears imprisoned in Rome, abandoned by many and sure that death is approaching: “I am already being poured out like a drink offering” (4:6). He writes to Timothy, whom he calls “my beloved child,” to encourage him not to be ashamed of the gospel, to endure hardship like a soldier, to pass the teaching on to faithful people and to remain in the Scriptures he has known since childhood, at a time of deceiving teachers. The last chapter, with the review of his own life and a series of requests and news about co-workers, is one of the most personal passages attributed to Paul.

Structure

  1. Not being ashamed ch. 1

    Paul recalls Timothy's faith, inherited from his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice, asks him to fan into flame the gift of God and not to be ashamed of the prisoner; he contrasts those who deserted him in Asia with Onesiphorus, who searched for him in Rome.

  2. Steadfastness in service ch. 2

    Images of the soldier, the athlete and the farmer; a saying about dying and living with Christ; avoiding useless quarrels, like those of Hymenaeus and Philetus, and correcting with gentleness.

  3. Difficult times and the Scriptures ch. 3

    A portrait of the wicked in the last days; Paul's example in persecutions; the Scriptures, inspired by God and useful for teaching and correction.

  4. Final charge and news ch. 4

    “Preach the word”; Paul sees his own life coming to an end; asks Timothy to come before winter, to bring Mark, the cloak and the parchments, and tells how he was left alone at his first defense.

Chapters

  1. 1 Not ashamed of the gospel
  2. 2 A soldier of Christ and an approved worker
  3. 3 Difficult times and the value of the Scriptures
  4. 4 Preach the word; the end of the race
