Ephesians Πρὸς Ἐφεσίους

Ephesians is less an answer to concrete problems than a broad reflection on what God has done in Christ and on the Church. The first half, in a tone of praise and prayer, speaks of God's plan to bring all things together in Christ, of salvation “by grace… through faith” (2:8) and of the union of Jews and Gentiles in one people, without “the dividing wall” (2:14). The second half draws the consequences: the unity of the body of Christ, the diversity of gifts, the abandonment of the old life, relationships within the household and the image of the “armor of God” for facing spiritual forces. Many expressions and themes coincide with those of Colossians.

Structure

  1. Greeting and praise ch. 1

    A long blessing (in the Greek, a single sentence, 1:3–14) celebrates election, redemption and the seal of the Spirit, followed by a prayer that the readers may understand the power of God shown in Christ.

  2. From dead to one people ch. 2

    Those who were dead in their sins receive life by grace; Gentiles once far off are brought near, and Jews and Gentiles form one new humanity.

  3. The mystery and Paul's mission ch. 3

    Paul, a prisoner, presents himself as a servant of the “mystery”: the Gentiles are fellow heirs. It ends with a prayer and a doxology.

  4. Unity and new life chs. 4–5

    One body and one Spirit, gifts given to build up the Church; putting off the “old self,” walking in love and as children of light (4:1–5:20).

  5. The household and the battle chs. 5–6

    Duties of wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters, with marriage compared to the union of Christ and the Church; the armor of God; Tychicus will bring news (5:21–6:24).

Chapters

  1. 1 Blessings in Christ; a prayer for wisdom
  2. 2 Saved by grace; Jews and Gentiles one people
  3. 3 The mystery revealed to the Gentiles
  4. 4 The unity of the body and the new life
  5. 5 Walking in love and in the light; husbands and wives
  6. 6 Children, servants and the armor of God
