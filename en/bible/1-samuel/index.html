1 Samuel שְׁמוּאֵל א

1 Samuel narrates Israel's passage from the time of the judges to the monarchy. The book begins with the birth of Samuel, son of Hannah, and with the fall of the house of the priest Eli, when the ark of God is captured by the Philistines and later returned. Samuel leads the people as judge and prophet until the elders ask for a king; Saul is anointed, wins the first battles, but disobeys and is rejected. The second half follows David: anointed in secret, he enters Saul's court, defeats Goliath, becomes Jonathan's friend and the target of the king's jealousy, and spends years as a fugitive. The book ends with the death of Saul and his sons on Mount Gilboa.

Structure

  1. Samuel and the end of the house of Eli chs. 1–7

    Samuel's birth and call, the loss and return of the ark, and the victory at Mizpah.

  2. The request for a king and Saul's reign chs. 8–15

    The people ask for a king, Saul is chosen and consolidates power, but ends up rejected after Gilgal and Amalek.

  3. David at Saul's court chs. 16–20

    David's anointing, the victory over Goliath, the friendship with Jonathan and Saul's attempts to kill him.

  4. David a fugitive and the end of Saul chs. 21–31

    David lives in the wilderness and among the Philistines and spares Saul's life twice; Saul dies at Gilboa.
