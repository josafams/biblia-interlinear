Habakkuk חֲבַקּוּק

Habakkuk is a dialogue between the prophet and God. The prophet complains of the violence and injustice in Judah; God answers that He will raise up the Chaldeans (Babylonians) as an instrument of judgment, which provokes a second complaint: how can He use a people even more violent? The answer includes the affirmation that the righteous will live by faith and a series of woes against the oppressor. The book ends with a prayer in the form of a psalm, which trusts in God even when everything is lacking.

Structure

  1. The dialogue with God chs. 1–2

    Two complaints of the prophet and two answers: the Chaldeans as an instrument of judgment and the vision that must be written down; then, five woes against the oppressor.

  2. The prophet's prayer ch. 3

    A psalm that describes God's coming as a warrior and ends with a declaration of trust.

Chapters

  1. 1 The prophet's complaint and the Chaldeans
  2. 2 God's answer and the five woes
  3. 3 The prayer of Habakkuk
