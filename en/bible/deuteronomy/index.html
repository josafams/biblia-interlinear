Deuteronomy דְּבָרִים

Deuteronomy presents itself as the words of Moses to Israel on the plains of Moab, in the last year of the wandering, shortly before the people cross the Jordan. Moses recalls the journey from Horeb, repeats the Ten Commandments and urges the people to love and serve the LORD alone. At the center is a long code of laws on worship, justice, family, economy and war, with emphasis on the demand for a single place of worship. The book ends with blessings and curses, the renewal of the covenant, the choice of Joshua as successor, the song and blessing of Moses and the account of his death on Mount Nebo.

Structure

  1. First speech: memory of the journey chs. 1–4

    From Horeb to the plains of Moab, with an appeal to faithfulness (1:1–4:43).

  2. Second speech: exhortation chs. 4–11

    The Ten Commandments, the “Hear, O Israel” and the memory of the wilderness and of the golden calf (4:44–11:32).

  3. Second speech: the law code chs. 12–26

    Place of worship, feasts, authorities, war, family, social justice and the firstfruits.

  4. Blessings and curses chs. 27–28

    The ceremony on Mounts Ebal and Gerizim and the consequences of obedience and disobedience.

  5. Third speech: the covenant in Moab chs. 29–30

    The renewal of the covenant and the choice between life and death.

  6. The last days of Moses chs. 31–34

    Joshua as successor, the law written and read, the song, the blessing of the tribes and the death of Moses.
