Numbers בְּמִדְבַּר

Numbers follows Israel from Mount Sinai to the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, across from Jericho. It begins with the census of the tribes and the organization of the camp around the tabernacle and, after the departure from Sinai, narrates a series of complaints and rebellions along the way. When the people refuse to enter Canaan after the spies' report, the generation that came out of Egypt is condemned to wander forty years in the wilderness. The last part takes place on the plains of Moab: Balaam's oracles, a new census of the next generation, the choice of Joshua and laws for life in the land. Narratives and laws alternate throughout the book.

Structure

  1. At Sinai chs. 1–10

    Census, the order of the camp, the duties of the Levites, purity laws and the dedication of the tabernacle, up to the departure in 10:11.

  2. On the way, through the wilderness chs. 10–21

    Complaints, the refusal before Canaan, Korah's rebellion, the deaths of Miriam and Aaron and the first victories east of the Jordan.

  3. On the plains of Moab chs. 22–36

    Balaam, Baal of Peor, the second census, Joshua as successor, the calendar of offerings, the war against Midian and the preparation for dividing the land.
