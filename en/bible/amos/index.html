Amos עָמוֹס

Amos, a herdsman from Judah, was sent to the Northern Kingdom in a time of prosperity. The book begins with oracles against the neighboring peoples and ends by pointing at Israel, accused of exploiting the poor, corrupting the courts and keeping an abundant and empty worship. A series of visions announces the end of the kingdom, and the confrontation with the priest of Bethel shows the resistance to the message. The final verses promise the restoration of the “tent of David.”

Structure

  1. Oracles against the nations chs. 1–2

    Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, Moab and Judah are judged, and the circle closes on Israel.

  2. Words against Israel chs. 3–6

    Being chosen brings responsibility; the luxury of Samaria, the oppression of the poor and worship without justice are denounced.

  3. Visions and restoration chs. 7–9

    Locusts, fire, plumb line, basket of fruit and the Lord beside the altar; the confrontation with Amaziah and the final promise.
