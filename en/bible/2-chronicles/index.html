2 Chronicles דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים

2 Chronicles continues the history begun in 1 Chronicles. Chapters 1 to 9 deal with the reign of Solomon, almost entirely devoted to the building and dedication of the temple. After the division of the kingdom, the book follows only the kings of Judah, from Rehoboam to the destruction of Jerusalem by the Babylonians, and assesses each one by his faithfulness to God and his care for the temple. Reforming kings, such as Asa, Jehoshaphat, Hezekiah and Josiah, receive a good deal of space. The book ends with the exile and with the decree of Cyrus, which authorizes the return to Jerusalem.

Structure

  1. Solomon and the temple chs. 1–9

    The wisdom asked for at Gibeon, the building and dedication of the temple, the riches of the kingdom and the visit of the queen of Sheba.

  2. The division and the first kings of Judah chs. 10–16

    Rehoboam, the separation of the northern tribes, Abijah and the long reign of Asa.

  3. Jehoshaphat chs. 17–20

    Teaching of the law, alliance with Ahab, organization of justice and the victory over Moab and Ammon.

  4. From Jehoram to Ahaz chs. 21–28

    A period of crises: the usurpation of Athaliah, the ambiguous reigns of Joash, Amaziah and Uzziah, and the idolatry of Ahaz.

  5. Hezekiah chs. 29–32

    The purification of the temple, the great Passover and the deliverance of Jerusalem from Sennacherib.

  6. From Manasseh to the exile chs. 33–36

    Manasseh and his repentance, Josiah's reform, the last kings, the destruction of Jerusalem and the decree of Cyrus.
