2 Samuel שְׁמוּאֵל ב

2 Samuel is the story of David's reign. After lamenting Saul and Jonathan, David reigns over Judah at Hebron, while Ish-bosheth, Saul's son, rules the other tribes; after a war between the two houses, David is recognized as king of all Israel, conquers Jerusalem and brings the ark there. Through the prophet Nathan, he receives the promise of a lasting dynasty. The adultery with Bathsheba and the death of Uriah mark a turning point: there follow crises in his own family, such as Amnon's violence against Tamar and Absalom's revolt, which forces David to flee Jerusalem. The last chapters gather episodes, lists and poems from various periods of the reign.

Structure

  1. David king at Hebron chs. 1–4

    The lament for Saul and Jonathan, the reign over Judah and the war with the house of Saul.

  2. David in Jerusalem chs. 5–10

    King of all Israel, David takes Jerusalem, brings up the ark, receives Nathan's promise and defeats his neighbors.

  3. Bathsheba and the crises of the reign chs. 11–20

    David's sin, Nathan's rebuke, the tragedy of Amnon and Tamar and the revolts of Absalom and Sheba.

  4. Appendices chs. 21–24

    Episodes, lists of warriors and two poems of David, outside chronological order.
