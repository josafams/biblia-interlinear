2 Kings מְלָכִים ב

2 Kings continues the history of the two kingdoms to the end of both. Elijah is taken up to heaven and Elisha takes his place, with a series of miracles and interventions in the politics of Israel and Aram. Jehu's coup eliminates Ahab's dynasty and the worship of Baal in the north, while in Judah Queen Athaliah usurps the throne until the crowning of the boy Joash. With the advance of Assyria, Samaria falls and much of Israel's population is deported, which chapter 17 interprets as a consequence of the people's unfaithfulness. Judah survives Sennacherib's siege in the time of Hezekiah, passes through the long reign of Manasseh and the reform of Josiah, but ends up subject to Babylon: Jerusalem and the temple are destroyed and the people go into exile. The book ends with King Jehoiachin, a prisoner in Babylon, being released and received at the king's table.

Structure

  1. Elisha and the end of the house of Ahab chs. 1–8

    Elijah is taken up to heaven; Elisha's miracles, the wars with Moab and with Aram.

  2. From Jehu to the fall of Samaria chs. 9–17

    Jehu's coup, Athaliah and Joash, the kings of the two kingdoms and the end of the kingdom of Israel.

  3. Judah, from Hezekiah to Josiah chs. 18–23

    Sennacherib's siege, the reign of Manasseh and the reform of Josiah.

  4. The last kings and the exile chs. 24–25

    Babylon dominates Judah, deports King Jehoiachin and destroys Jerusalem and the temple.
