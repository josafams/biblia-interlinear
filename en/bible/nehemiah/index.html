Nehemiah נְחֶמְיָה

Nehemiah, cupbearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes in Susa, receives news of Jerusalem's precarious situation and obtains permission to rebuild the city's walls. Despite the opposition of Sanballat, Tobiah and Geshem, the work is completed in fifty-two days. The book also narrates the public reading of the law by Ezra, the people's confession and the commitment signed by the community, the repopulation of Jerusalem and the dedication of the walls. It ends with Nehemiah's reforms in a second term as governor: the Sabbath, the tithes, the temple service and marriages with foreigners.

Structure

  1. Nehemiah's call chs. 1–2

    The news about Jerusalem, the prayer, the request to the king and the night inspection of the walls.

  2. Rebuilding the wall chs. 3–7

    The groups of builders, the armed opposition and the intrigues, the debt crisis, the completion of the wall and the list of the returned exiles.

  3. The law and the covenant chs. 8–10

    Ezra reads the law before the people, the Feast of Tabernacles, the great confession and the written commitment.

  4. Jerusalem repopulated chs. 11–12

    The city's new residents, the lists of priests and Levites and the celebration of the dedication of the wall.

  5. The final reforms ch. 13

    On his return to Jerusalem, Nehemiah corrects abuses in the temple, on the Sabbath and in marriages.
