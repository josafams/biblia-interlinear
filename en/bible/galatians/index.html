Galatians Πρὸς Γαλάτας

Galatians is a letter written under tension. After Paul had passed through the region, teachers arrived in the churches of Galatia who demanded that non-Jewish Christians be circumcised and keep the Law of Moses, and Paul reacts without the thanksgiving with which he usually opens his letters: he is amazed that the Galatians have so quickly turned to “a different gospel” (1:6). In the first two chapters he tells his own story to show that his gospel did not come from men, including the agreement with the leaders in Jerusalem and the confrontation with Cephas in Antioch. In chapters 3 and 4 he argues from the story of Abraham and from the Scriptures that righteousness comes by faith, and that the Law had a provisional role. The final part speaks of freedom in Christ, which must not become a pretext for the flesh, and of life led by the Spirit.

Structure

  1. Opening and rebuke ch. 1

    Paul affirms that he is an apostle not by human choice and, instead of giving thanks, rebukes the Galatians for listening to those who pervert the gospel (1:1–10).

  2. Paul's story chs. 1–2

    The former persecutor receives the revelation of Christ, goes to Jerusalem only years later, obtains from the leaders there the recognition of his mission among the Gentiles and confronts Cephas in Antioch over table fellowship with non-Jews (1:11–2:21).

  3. Faith, Abraham and the Law chs. 3–4

    The Galatians received the Spirit by faith; Abraham believed and was counted righteous; the Law came later, as a guardian until Christ; now all are sons and heirs, not slaves. The comparison between Hagar and Sarah closes the argument.

  4. Freedom and life in the Spirit chs. 5–6

    Whoever lets himself be circumcised to be justified returns to the yoke; freedom is lived out in love, and the works of the flesh are contrasted with the fruit of the Spirit. Practical counsel and a closing written in his own hand (6:11–18).

Chapters

  1. 1 No other gospel; Paul's call
  2. 2 Agreement in Jerusalem; conflict with Cephas in Antioch
  3. 3 The faith of Abraham and the purpose of the Law
  4. 4 Sons, not slaves; the slave woman and the free woman
  5. 5 Freedom, love and the fruit of the Spirit
  6. 6 Bearing burdens; sowing and reaping
