Job אִיּוֹב

Job deals with the suffering of a righteous man and the question of how it relates to God's justice. A prose prologue introduces Job, rich and upright, who in a short time loses his possessions, his children and his health, after a conversation in heaven in which the accuser questions the reason for his faithfulness. Three friends come to comfort him, and the meeting turns into a long debate in poetry: they maintain that suffering is the consequence of sin, and Job insists on his own innocence and asks to present his case before God. After the speeches of a fourth character, Elihu, the LORD Himself speaks out of a whirlwind, showing Job creation and its mysteries. Job yields, the friends are rebuked, and a prose epilogue tells of the restoration of his life.

Structure

  1. Prologue chs. 1–2

    Job is introduced as upright and prosperous; in two scenes in heaven, the accuser questions his faithfulness, and Job loses possessions, children and health. Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar arrive.

  2. Job's lament ch. 3

    After seven days of silence, Job curses the day of his birth and asks why life is given to those who suffer.

  3. First cycle of dialogues chs. 4–14

    Each friend speaks and Job answers each one: the friends argue that God rewards the righteous and punishes the wicked; Job rejects this explanation for his own case.

  4. Second cycle of dialogues chs. 15–21

    The friends insist on the fate reserved for the wicked; Job feels abandoned, but affirms that he has a witness in heaven and that he will see God.

  5. Third cycle of dialogues chs. 22–27

    Eliphaz accuses Job of concrete wrongs; Bildad says little, and Zophar does not speak again. Job describes the injustice in the world and maintains his integrity.

  6. Poem of wisdom ch. 28

    Human beings know how to extract metals from the depths, but cannot find the way of wisdom, which only God knows.

  7. Job's final defense chs. 29–31

    Job compares the honor of former days with his present humiliation and ends with an oath of innocence, challenging God to answer.

  8. The speeches of Elihu chs. 32–37

    A young man, not mentioned until now, criticizes Job and the friends and presents suffering as a way God warns and instructs.

  9. The speeches of the LORD chs. 38–42

    Out of a whirlwind, God questions Job about the order of the cosmos, the wild animals, Behemoth and Leviathan; Job answers twice, the second time in 42:1–6.

  10. Epilogue ch. 42

    God rebukes the three friends, Job intercedes for them and receives double what he had lost (42:7–17).
