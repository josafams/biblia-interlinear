Acts Πράξεις Ἀποστόλων

The Acts of the Apostles continues the Gospel of Luke and narrates about thirty years of the Christian movement, from Jesus' departure in Jerusalem to the arrival of Paul, a prisoner, in Rome. The plan appears right at the beginning: the disciples would be witnesses “in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth” (1:8). The first part follows the community in Jerusalem, led by Peter, with Pentecost, the death of Stephen and the welcome of the first non-Jews, such as the centurion Cornelius. From chapter 13 on, the focus shifts to Paul and his journeys through Asia Minor and Greece, up to his arrest in Jerusalem, the trials before Roman authorities and the sea voyage to Rome. Throughout the book, the Holy Spirit drives the expansion, and speeches by Peter, Stephen and Paul present the message to different audiences.

Structure

  1. Jerusalem chs. 1–7

    The ascension, the choice of Matthias, Pentecost and Peter's speech, the life of the community, the conflicts with the temple authorities, the choice of the Seven and the death of Stephen.

  2. Judea, Samaria and the first Gentiles chs. 8–12

    With the scattering, Philip preaches in Samaria and baptizes an Ethiopian; Saul is converted on the road to Damascus; Peter baptizes Cornelius; in Antioch a church with many Gentiles arises, where the disciples are first called Christians (11:26).

  3. First journey and the Jerusalem council chs. 13–15

    Barnabas and Saul set out from Antioch for Cyprus and southern Asia Minor; back again, the demand to circumcise the Gentiles is debated by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem (ch. 15).

  4. Second and third journeys chs. 15–21

    Paul takes the message to Macedonia and Greece (Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens and Corinth), spends about three years in Ephesus, says farewell to the elders at Miletus and goes up to Jerusalem (15:36–21:16).

  5. Paul a prisoner chs. 21–26

    Arrested in the temple, Paul defends himself before the people, the Council, the governors Felix and Festus and King Agrippa, and appeals to Caesar.

  6. The voyage to Rome chs. 27–28

    The crossing, the shipwreck at Malta and the arrival in Rome, where Paul spends two years in a rented house, preaching “with all boldness and without hindrance” (28:31).
