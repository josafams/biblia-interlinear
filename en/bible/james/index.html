James Ἰακώβου

James is a set of practical teachings addressed to “the twelve tribes of the Dispersion” (1:1). In short sentences and images drawn from everyday life (the mirror, the horse's bit, the ship's rudder, the flower that withers, the farmer who waits for the rain), it deals with trials, controlling the tongue, favoritism toward the rich, the exploitation of workers, arrogance in making plans and prayer. The best-known passage states that faith without deeds is dead (2:14–26). There is almost no doctrinal exposition: the interest is in a faith that shows itself in conduct and in care for the poor.

Structure

  1. Trials and the word ch. 1

    Trials produce perseverance; wisdom is asked of God; temptation is born of one's own desire; the word must be done and not merely heard.

  2. Favoritism and faith with deeds ch. 2

    Honoring the rich and despising the poor goes against the law of love; a faith that does not act, like that of someone who merely wishes the needy well, is dead; Abraham and Rahab are the examples.

  3. The tongue and wisdom chs. 3–4

    The tongue, small like a bit or a rudder, can set everything on fire; the wisdom from above is peaceable, while envy and ambition breed quarrels; whoever wants to be a friend of the world opposes God (3:1–4:10).

  4. Judgments, plans and riches chs. 4–5

    Not speaking against a brother, not making plans as if tomorrow were guaranteed; a denunciation of the rich who withhold the workers' wages (4:11–5:6).

  5. Patience and prayer ch. 5

    Waiting for the coming of the Lord as the farmer waits for the harvest, not swearing oaths, praying for the sick, confessing faults to one another and bringing back the one who has strayed (5:7–20).

Chapters

  1. 1 Trials, wisdom and doing the word
  2. 2 Favoritism; faith without works is dead
  3. 3 The tongue and the wisdom from above
  4. 4 Quarrels, friendship with the world and arrogance
  5. 5 A warning to the rich; patience and prayer
