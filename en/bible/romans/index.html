Romans Πρὸς Ῥωμαίους

Romans is Paul's longest letter and his most systematic. Written to a church he did not yet know, it presents his gospel as “the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, first to the Jew, then to the Greek” (1:16). Chapters 1 to 8 develop the argument: Jews and non-Jews are equally under sin, God makes righteous those who believe, as He did with Abraham, and gives a new life, led by the Spirit. Chapters 9 to 11 face the question of Israel's place, since most of Paul's people had not accepted Jesus as Messiah. The final part deals with life in community, the relationship with the authorities and living together among those who think differently about food and holy days, and ends with Paul's travel plans and a long list of greetings.

Structure

  1. Opening and theme ch. 1

    Paul introduces himself, speaks of his long-held desire to visit Rome and announces the subject: in the gospel the righteousness of God is revealed, received by faith (1:1–17).

  2. All under sin chs. 1–3

    The peoples exchanged the Creator for idols, and those who judge others do the same; Jews and Greeks stand equally before God's judgment (1:18–3:20).

  3. Justified by faith chs. 3–5

    God freely makes righteous those who believe in Jesus; Abraham is the example, and Christ undoes what began with Adam (3:21–5:21).

  4. New life chs. 6–8

    United with Christ through baptism, those who believe die to sin; the Law is good, but does not set free, and the one who sets free is the Spirit, who makes them children of God and sustains them in hope.

  5. Israel in God's plan chs. 9–11

    The refusal of much of Israel does not cancel the promises: there is a remnant, the Gentiles have been grafted into the olive tree, and Paul hopes that “all Israel” will be saved.

  6. The life of the community chs. 12–15

    Worship as the offering of one's whole life, the gifts, love without hypocrisy, the authorities and the duty to welcome those with different convictions (12:1–15:13).

  7. Plans and greetings chs. 15–16

    Paul explains his work among the Gentiles, the trip to Jerusalem with the collection and the plan to go on to Spain, and sends greetings to dozens of people (15:14–16:27).

Chapters

  1. 1 Greeting; the gospel and human idolatry
  2. 2 God's impartial judgment; circumcision of the heart
  3. 3 All have sinned; righteousness through faith in Christ
  4. 4 The example of Abraham: faith and righteousness
  5. 5 Peace with God; Adam and Christ
  6. 6 Baptism, grace and serving righteousness
  7. 7 The Law and the inner conflict
  8. 8 The Spirit, hope and unconquerable love
  9. 9 Paul's sorrow; promise and election
  10. 10 Righteousness by faith, within everyone's reach
  11. 11 Israel not rejected; the olive tree and the branches
  12. 12 A renewed mind, gifts and sincere love
  13. 13 The authorities and love of neighbor
  14. 14 Food, holy days and the weaker brother
  15. 15 Accept one another; travel plans
  16. 16 Phoebe, greetings and final warnings
