Genesis בְּרֵאשִׁית

Genesis tells of origins: of the world, of humanity and of the people of Israel. Chapters 1 to 11 narrate the creation, the disobedience in the garden of Eden, the flood and the scattering of the peoples at Babel. From chapter 12 on, the story focuses on one family: Abraham receives from God the promise of land, descendants and blessing, and this promise passes to Isaac, to Jacob and to Jacob's twelve sons. The book ends with the family living in Egypt, which sets the stage for Exodus.

Structure

  1. Origins of the world and of humanity chs. 1–11

    Creation, Eden, Cain and Abel, the genealogies, the flood, the covenant with Noah and the tower of Babel.

  2. Abraham chs. 12–25

    The call, the promise, the covenant and the birth of Isaac.

  3. Isaac and Jacob chs. 25–36

    Esau and Jacob, the blessing, Jacob's years with Laban and the new name, Israel.

  4. Joseph chs. 37–50

    Sold by his brothers, Joseph rises to governor of Egypt and is reunited with his family.
