Jeremiah יִרְמְיָהוּ

Jeremiah gathers the messages and the story of a prophet who was active in Jerusalem during the last four decades of the kingdom of Judah, from about 627 BC until after the destruction of the city by the Babylonians, in 587/586 BC. He denounces idolatry and injustice, attacks blind trust in the temple and insists that Judah must submit to Babylon, which makes him seen as a traitor, imprisoned and threatened with death. The book also preserves prayers in which the prophet complains to God about the weight of his mission, the accounts of his imprisonments and of the fall of Jerusalem, and the forced flight of a group of Jews, with Jeremiah, to Egypt. Amid the announcements of judgment, there are promises of return from exile and of a new covenant written on the heart (chs. 30–33). The book ends with oracles against the nations and a historical appendix on the fall of Jerusalem.

Structure

  1. Call and warnings to Judah chs. 1–25

    The prophet's call, denunciations of idolatry and injustice, the temple sermon, symbolic acts, Jeremiah's personal complaints and the announcement of the seventy years of Babylonian rule.

  2. Conflicts with priests, prophets and kings chs. 26–29

    Jeremiah is put on trial, confronts the prophet Hananiah over the yoke of Babylon and writes to the exiles.

  3. Promises of restoration chs. 30–33

    Poems of comfort, the new covenant and the purchase of a field at Anathoth as a sign that life will return to the land.

  4. Jerusalem's last years chs. 34–45

    The scroll burned by the king, the prophet's imprisonments, the fall of the city, the murder of the governor Gedaliah and the flight to Egypt.

  5. Oracles against the nations chs. 46–51

    Egypt, Philistia, Moab, Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar, Elam and, last and at greatest length, Babylon.

  6. Historical appendix ch. 52

    The fall of Jerusalem and the release of King Jehoiachin in Babylon, in a text close to 2 Kings 24–25.
