1 Kings מְלָכִים א

1 Kings begins with David's old age and the dispute over the succession, won by Solomon. Solomon's reign is presented as a time of wisdom, wealth and great works, with the building and dedication of the temple in Jerusalem, but also of marriages to foreign women and the worship of other gods. After his death the kingdom divides: Rehoboam keeps Judah, in the south, and Jeroboam founds the kingdom of Israel, in the north, with sanctuaries at Bethel and Dan. From then on the book alternates between the kings of the two kingdoms, assessing each one by faithfulness to the LORD. The final chapters revolve around the prophet Elijah and his confrontation with King Ahab and Queen Jezebel, who promoted the worship of Baal.

Structure

  1. David's succession chs. 1–2

    Adonijah tries to take the throne, Solomon is anointed and David dies.

  2. Solomon's reign chs. 3–11

    The king's wisdom, the temple and the palace, the riches and, at the end, the idolatry and the adversaries.

  3. The division and the first kings chs. 12–16

    The kingdom splits in two; kings of Judah and Israel down to Ahab.

  4. Elijah and Ahab chs. 17–22

    The drought, Carmel, Horeb, Naboth's vineyard and Ahab's death at Ramoth-gilead.
