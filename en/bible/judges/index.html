Judges שֹׁפְטִים

Judges narrates the life of the tribes of Israel in Canaan between the death of Joshua and the rise of the monarchy. The book begins by showing that the conquest remained incomplete and presents a cycle that repeats itself: the people turn to other gods, fall into the hands of an oppressor, cry out to the LORD and receive a deliverer, until it all begins again. Into this pattern come the stories of Othniel, Ehud, Deborah and Barak, Gideon, Jephthah and Samson, besides judges mentioned in a few lines. With each turn the situation worsens, and the final chapters, marked by the phrase “there was no king in Israel,” tell episodes of idolatry, violence and a war among the tribes themselves.

Structure

  1. An unfinished conquest chs. 1–3

    The tribes advance, but many Canaanites remain (1:1–2:5); after Joshua's death, a new generation turns away from the LORD, and the text presents the cycle that will repeat (2:6–3:6).

  2. The first deliverers chs. 3–5

    Othniel, Ehud and Shamgar; Deborah and Barak defeat Sisera's army, and a song celebrates the victory.

  3. Gideon and Abimelech chs. 6–9

    Gideon defeats the Midianites with three hundred men and refuses the crown; his son Abimelech makes himself king in Shechem and ends up destroyed together with the city.

  4. Jephthah and other judges chs. 10–12

    Tola and Jair, the oppression of the Ammonites, Jephthah's victory and vow, the conflict with Ephraim and the judges Ibzan, Elon and Abdon.

  5. Samson chs. 13–16

    The Nazirite from the tribe of Dan faces the Philistines alone until he is betrayed by Delilah and dies bringing down the temple of Dagon, in Gaza.

  6. When there was no king chs. 17–21

    Two final episodes: Micah's shrine and the migration of the tribe of Dan; the crime at Gibeah and the war that almost wipes out the tribe of Benjamin.
