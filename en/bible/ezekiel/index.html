Ezekiel יְחֶזְקֵאל

Ezekiel is the book of a priest taken to Babylon in the deportation of 597 BC, who received his prophetic call among the exiles, by the Chebar River. Until the fall of Jerusalem, in 587/586 BC, his message is mainly one of judgment: in visions, symbolic acts and allegories, he shows that the city and the temple will be destroyed because of idolatry and violence, and that the glory of God leaves the sanctuary. Then come oracles against neighboring nations, especially Tyre and Egypt. After the news of the fall arrives, the tone changes to hope: God promises to be the shepherd of the people Himself, to give a new heart, to bring the dry bones to life and to gather Israel and Judah. The book ends with a long vision of a renewed temple, of a river that flows from it and gives life, and of a new division of the land.

Structure

  1. The prophet's call chs. 1–3

    The vision of God's glory above a chariot of living creatures and wheels, the scroll the prophet eats and the mission of watchman.

  2. Judgment on Jerusalem chs. 4–24

    Acts that enact the siege, the vision of the abominations in the temple and of the glory that departs, allegories of Israel's history and the announcement of the start of the siege.

  3. Oracles against the nations chs. 25–32

    Ammon, Moab, Edom and the Philistines, and long poems about Tyre and Egypt.

  4. Israel's restoration chs. 33–39

    After the news of the fall: the watchman, the good shepherd, the new heart, the valley of dry bones and the defeat of Gog.

  5. The new temple and the new land chs. 40–48

    A detailed vision of the temple, the return of the glory, rules for worship, the river that flows from the sanctuary and the division of the land among the tribes.
