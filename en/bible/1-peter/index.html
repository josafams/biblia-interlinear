1 Peter Πέτρου Αʹ

1 Peter is a letter of encouragement to Christians scattered through the provinces of northern and central Asia Minor, called “exiles” (1:1). They suffered suspicion, slander and hostility for having left the customs of the society in which they lived. The letter reminds them that they have been born again to a living hope, that they form the people of God (“a royal priesthood, a holy nation,” 2:9) and that unjust suffering, endured while doing good, unites them to Christ, who also suffered. It gives guidance for life in society and at home, for the elders who care for the communities and for watchfulness against the adversary.

Structure

  1. A people born again chs. 1–2

    Praise for the living hope, joy amid trials, a call to holiness and love, and the image of the living stones that form a spiritual house (1:1–2:10).

  2. Conduct among outsiders chs. 2–3

    Living so that accusers may see good deeds: respect for the authorities, counsel to household slaves, to wives and to husbands, and the example of Christ who suffered without retaliating (2:11–3:12).

  3. Suffering for doing good chs. 3–4

    Being ready to give a reason for the hope with gentleness; Christ suffered for the unrighteous; whoever suffers “as a Christian” should not be ashamed (3:13–4:19).

  4. Elders and farewell ch. 5

    The elders must shepherd without greed or domineering; all must be humble and watchful; greetings from Silvanus, from Mark and from the church “in Babylon.”

Chapters

  1. 1 A living hope and a call to holiness
  2. 2 Living stones; conduct among the pagans
  3. 3 Life at home; suffering for doing good
  4. 4 Suffering as a Christian; serving with gifts
  5. 5 To the elders; humility and watchfulness
