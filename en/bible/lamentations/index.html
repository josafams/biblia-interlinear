Lamentations אֵיכָה

Lamentations gathers five poems of mourning for the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple by the Babylonians, in 587 or 586 BC. The poems describe the siege, the famine, the death of children, humiliation before the enemies and the exile, and give a voice to the city itself, portrayed as a widow who weeps with no one to comfort her. The catastrophe is understood as the consequence of the sins of the people and their leaders, but the text does not hide the pain or the questions addressed to God. At the center of the book (3:21–33), the complaint opens into hope in the loving devotion and faithfulness of the LORD, and the last poem ends with a prayer for restoration.

Structure

  1. The widowed city ch. 1

    The poet describes Jerusalem abandoned, and the city itself speaks to ask the LORD to see its pain.

  2. The Lord's anger ch. 2

    God is described acting like an enemy: He tears down walls, palaces and the sanctuary, while children faint from hunger in the streets.

  3. The man who has seen affliction ch. 3

    A sufferer speaks in the first person; from complaint he passes to hope in the LORD's mercies and to a call to repentance.

  4. Before and after the siege ch. 4

    The contrast between past glory and present misery, the guilt of prophets and priests, the capture of the king and the announcement of punishment on Edom.

  5. The community's prayer ch. 5

    The people, in the first person plural, describe their humiliation and ask the LORD to restore them and renew their days.

Chapters

  1. 1 Jerusalem, the widowed city without comfort
  2. 2 The Lord acted like an enemy
  3. 3 From affliction to hope in His mercy
  4. 4 Hunger and ruin in the besieged city
  5. 5 The people's prayer for restoration
