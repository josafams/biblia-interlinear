Ezra עֶזְרָא

Ezra tells of the return of the Jews exiled in Babylon and the rebuilding of religious life in Jerusalem. Chapters 1 to 6 run from the decree of Cyrus, in 538 BC, to the completion of the temple, in 515 BC, passing through the opposition of the neighboring peoples, which halts the work for some years. After an interval of several decades, chapters 7 to 10 narrate the arrival of Ezra, priest and scribe, with a letter from King Artaxerxes authorizing him to teach and apply the law of Moses. The book ends with the crisis of the marriages with foreign women and the decision to dissolve them.

Structure

  1. The return from exile chs. 1–2

    The decree of Cyrus, the return of the temple articles and the list of those who came back.

  2. The rebuilding of the temple chs. 3–6

    The altar and the foundations, the opposition and the halt, the resumption with Haggai and Zechariah and the dedication of the temple.

  3. Ezra's mission chs. 7–8

    Ezra's genealogy, Artaxerxes' letter and the journey from Babylon to Jerusalem.

  4. The mixed marriages chs. 9–10

    Ezra's prayer, the assembly of the people and the list of those who separated from their foreign wives.
