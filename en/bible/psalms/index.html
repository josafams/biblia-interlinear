Psalms תְּהִלִּים

Psalms is Israel's great collection of poems of prayer and song: 150 texts that speak to God and speak of God. There are hymns that celebrate the Creator and the King of all the earth, laments of individuals and of the people in danger, thanksgivings for deliverance, psalms about Israel's king and about Zion, meditations on the Law and reflections of wisdom. Many voices cross: the complaint of one who feels abandoned, the confession of guilt, trust, the joy of the festival and even the plea for vengeance against oppressors. The collection is divided into five books and moves, as a whole, from lament to praise, ending in a series of psalms that begin and end with “Hallelujah.”

Structure

  1. Book I chs. 1–41

    Opens with two introductory psalms, on the two ways (1) and on the anointed king (2). Almost all the others bear David's name in the title, and individual pleas predominate. It ends with a blessing (41:13).

  2. Book II chs. 42–72

    Begins with psalms of the sons of Korah (42–49), continues with one of Asaph (50) and another group attributed to David (51–71). It ends with a psalm for the king, a blessing and the note “the prayers of David son of Jesse are concluded” (72:18–20).

  3. Book III chs. 73–89

    Gathers psalms of Asaph (73–83) and of the sons of Korah (84–88), with many laments over the destruction of the sanctuary and the crisis of the people. It ends with Psalm 89, which asks about the covenant with David, and a blessing (89:52).

  4. Book IV chs. 90–106

    Opens with the prayer attributed to Moses (90) and gathers the psalms that proclaim that the LORD reigns (93–99). Psalms 105 and 106 retell Israel's history; the closing blessing is in 106:48.

  5. Book V chs. 107–150

    Includes the long psalm of the Law (119), the Songs of Ascents (120–134), a last group attributed to David (138–145) and the five “Hallelujah” psalms that close the collection (146–150).
