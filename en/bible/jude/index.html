Jude Ἰούδα

Jude is a short and forceful letter. The author intended to write about the salvation we share, but changed subject to warn against people who had slipped into the community and turned grace into a pretext for immorality. He draws on examples of judgment from the Scriptures and from Jewish traditions (the people in the wilderness, the rebellious angels, Sodom and Gomorrah, Cain, Balaam and Korah) and quotes a prophecy attributed to Enoch. He closes with counsel of mercy and with one of the best-known doxologies of the New Testament.

Structure

  1. Greeting and reason ch. 1

    Jude greets those who are called and explains why he writes: it is necessary to contend for the faith, because ungodly people have crept in unnoticed (1:1–4).

  2. Examples of judgment ch. 1

    The people who did not believe in the wilderness, the angels who abandoned their dwelling and Sodom and Gomorrah show that God judges (1:5–7).

  3. Portrait of the intruders ch. 1

    Compared to Cain, Balaam and Korah, to clouds without water and trees without fruit; Enoch's prophecy announces judgment on them (1:8–16).

  4. Exhortation and doxology ch. 1

    Remembering the words of the apostles, building oneself up in the faith, showing mercy to those who doubt and praising God, who is able to keep from stumbling (1:17–25).
