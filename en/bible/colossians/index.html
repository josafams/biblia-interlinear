Colossians Πρὸς Κολοσσαεῖς

Colossians is addressed to a church Paul neither founded nor visited, formed through the work of Epaphras. The center of the letter is the person of Christ, presented in a hymnic passage as “the image of the invisible God,” through whom all things were created and reconciled (1:15–20). From this comes the warning against a teaching the readers were hearing, made up of “philosophy,” rules about food and holy days, “the worship of angels” and visions (2:8–23): for the author, none of this adds to what one already has in Christ. The second part speaks of the new life, of relationships within the household and ends with a long list of news and greetings.

Structure

  1. Gratitude and prayer ch. 1

    Thanksgiving for the faith of the Colossians, who learned the gospel from Epaphras, and a prayer that they may grow in the knowledge of God's will (1:1–14).

  2. Christ and Paul's mission chs. 1–2

    Christ, firstborn over creation and from the dead, reconciles all things; Paul rejoices to suffer for the Church and struggles for those who do not know him personally (1:15–2:5).

  3. Against the rival teaching ch. 2

    In Christ all the fullness dwells; the readers were circumcised in Him and buried with Him in baptism; rules about eating, festivals and the worship of angels are “a shadow of the things to come” (2:6–23).

  4. The new life chs. 3–4

    Seeking the things above, putting off the old life and putting on compassion and love; duties of wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters; persevering in prayer (3:1–4:6).

  5. News and greetings ch. 4

    Tychicus and Onesimus will bring news; greetings from Aristarchus, Mark, Epaphras, Luke and Demas; an exchange of letters with Laodicea and a signature in Paul's own hand (4:7–18).

Chapters

  1. 1 Christ, the image of the invisible God
  2. 2 Fullness in Christ against human rules
  3. 3 The things above and life at home
  4. 4 Prayer, news and final greetings
