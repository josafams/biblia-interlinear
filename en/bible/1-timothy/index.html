1 Timothy Πρὸς Τιμόθεον Αʹ

1 Timothy is a letter of instructions to a co-worker charged with caring for the church in Ephesus while Paul goes on to Macedonia (1:3). It begins and ends with warnings against teachers who teach “false doctrine,” linked to genealogies, arguments about the Law and the prohibition of marriage and of certain foods. In between, it deals with the organization of the community: public prayer, the conduct of men and women in worship, the qualifications required of overseers and deacons, the care of widows, the pay and discipline of elders, the situation of slaves and the danger of love of money. It also contains personal advice to the young Timothy.

Structure

  1. Against false doctrine ch. 1

    Timothy must restrain those who get lost in myths and genealogies; Paul recalls that he was a blasphemer and persecutor and received mercy, and mentions Hymenaeus and Alexander as examples of shipwreck in the faith.

  2. Order in worship and leaders chs. 2–3

    Prayers for all, including rulers; instructions to men and women; qualifications of overseers and deacons; the church as “the pillar and foundation of the truth,” and a short hymn to Christ (3:16).

  3. False teachings and Timothy's conduct ch. 4

    Against those who forbid marriage and foods created by God; Timothy must be an example, devote himself to reading and teaching and not neglect the gift he received.

  4. Groups in the church chs. 5–6

    How to treat older and younger people, the support and enrollment of widows, the honor and correction of elders, the duty of slaves (5:1–6:2).

  5. Wealth and final charge ch. 6

    The love of money as a root of evils, counsel to the rich and the final appeal: fight the good fight of the faith and guard what has been entrusted (6:3–21).
