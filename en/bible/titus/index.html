Titus Πρὸς Τίτον

Titus is a brief letter to a co-worker left in Crete to organize the churches and appoint elders “in every town” (1:5). It describes the qualifications required of these leaders and asks for firmness against “rebellious” teachers, especially those of the circumcision group, interested in myths and purity rules. It then directs what to teach each group (older men, younger men, women, slaves) and grounds this behavior in the grace of God, which has appeared and trains us for a sober and upright life. It ends with the reminder that salvation comes from God's mercy, not from works, and with travel instructions.

Structure

  1. Leaders and opponents ch. 1

    A long greeting; Titus must appoint blameless elders and silence those who teach for dishonest gain, with an unflattering quotation about the Cretans (1:12).

  2. Teaching for each group ch. 2

    Counsel to older men, older women, young people and slaves, grounded in the grace of God that has appeared and in the hope of Christ's coming.

  3. Public life and salvation ch. 3

    Respect for the authorities and gentleness toward all, because God saved by mercy, through “the washing of new birth”; avoiding disputes; requests about Artemas, Tychicus, Zenas and Apollos.

Chapters

  1. 1 Elders in Crete and the rebellious
  2. 2 Teaching for each group; grace has appeared
  3. 3 Good works and renewal by the Spirit

Key verses

For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to everyone. It instructs us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live sensible, upright, and godly lives in the present age, as we await the blessed hope and glorious appearance of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ.

Titus 2:11–13

But when the kindness of God our Savior and His love for mankind appeared, He saved us, not by the righteous deeds we had done, but according to His mercy, through the washing of new birth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.

Titus 3:4–5
