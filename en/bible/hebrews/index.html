Hebrews Πρὸς Ἑβραίους

Hebrews is a long appeal to Christians tempted to lose heart and abandon the faith. The author argues that God, after speaking in many ways through the prophets, has spoken definitively through the Son, superior to the angels and to Moses. The center of the book presents Jesus as high priest “in the order of Melchizedek,” who entered the heavenly sanctuary with His own blood and offered a single sacrifice, inaugurating the new covenant announced by Jeremiah. Between the parts of the exposition come serious warnings and encouragement, and chapter 11 runs through the history of Israel recalling those who lived by faith. The book ends with practical counsel and greetings, like a letter.

Structure

  1. The Son, God's final word chs. 1–4

    The Son is greater than the angels and than Moses; those who have heard the message are called not to harden their hearts, like the wilderness generation, and to enter God's rest (1:1–4:13).

  2. The high priest chs. 4–7

    Jesus is the high priest who sympathizes with human weaknesses; after an appeal to maturity, the author compares Him to Melchizedek, a priest without genealogy and forever (4:14–7:28).

  3. New covenant and single sacrifice chs. 8–10

    The earthly sanctuary was a copy and shadow; Christ entered the heavenly sanctuary and, with a single offering, does what the repeated sacrifices could not do (8:1–10:18).

  4. Persevering in faith chs. 10–12

    A call to draw near to God with confidence, a warning against falling away, the gallery of those who lived by faith, the race with eyes fixed on Jesus and the contrast between Sinai and Mount Zion (10:19–12:29).

  5. Final exhortations ch. 13

    Brotherly love, hospitality, care for prisoners, faithfulness in marriage, respect for leaders, a request for prayer and greetings.

Key verses

For the word of God is living and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it pierces even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow. It judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

Hebrews 4:12

For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin. Let us then approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need.

Hebrews 4:15–16

Now faith is the assurance of what we hope for and the certainty of what we do not see.

Hebrews 11:1

Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a great cloud of witnesses, let us throw off every encumbrance and the sin that so easily entangles, and let us run with endurance the race set out for us. Let us fix our eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.
