Philippians Πρὸς Φιλιππησίους

Philippians is the letter of Paul most marked by affection. In prison and not knowing whether he will be released or condemned, he thanks the church in Philippi for the help it sent him through Epaphroditus and tells how his imprisonment has served to spread the gospel. He asks for unity and humility, presenting the example of Christ, who “emptied Himself” and was obedient to death on a cross (2:5–11). In chapter 3 he changes tone to warn against those who insist on circumcision and recalls his own story: everything he had counted as gain came to be worth little compared with knowing Christ. Joy, even in the midst of imprisonment, runs through the whole letter.

Structure

  1. Gratitude and news from prison ch. 1

    Paul gives thanks for the Philippians' partnership, tells that his chains have become known and that others preach more boldly, and weighs departing to be with Christ against remaining with them.

  2. Unity and the example of Christ chs. 1–2

    A call to live in a manner worthy of the gospel and with the same mind as Christ, who humbled Himself and was exalted; plans to send Timothy and the return of Epaphroditus, who was near death (1:27–2:30).

  3. Warning and the goal ch. 3

    Against those who put confidence in the flesh, Paul lists his own credentials and says he counts them as loss; he has not yet reached the end, but presses on toward the goal, as a citizen of heaven.

  4. Counsel and thanks ch. 4

    A plea for reconciliation between Euodia and Syntyche, an invitation to joy and prayer, and a careful thank-you for the gift received, with greetings from “the household of Caesar.”

Chapters

  1. 1 Paul's imprisonment and the advance of the gospel
  2. 2 The example of Christ; Timothy and Epaphroditus
  3. 3 Losing everything to gain Christ
  4. 4 Joy, peace and thanks for the gift
