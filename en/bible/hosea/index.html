Hosea הוֹשֵׁעַ

Hosea prophesied in the Northern Kingdom, Israel, in the last decades before its fall to Assyria. Chapters 1 to 3 use the prophet's own marriage to an unfaithful woman as an image of the relationship between God and Israel, which turns to Baal and other gods. Chapters 4 to 14 gather oracles against idolatry, the corruption of priests and kings and the alliances with Egypt and Assyria. Despite the accusations, the book insists on God's persistent love, which calls the people back and promises to heal them.

Structure

  1. The prophet's marriage chs. 1–3

    Hosea marries Gomer, gives his children names that announce judgment and later buys back his wife, in an image of Israel's unfaithfulness and God's faithfulness.

  2. The charge against Israel chs. 4–10

    Lack of knowledge of God, idolatry, kings made without consulting God and alliances with foreign powers.

  3. The love that does not give up chs. 11–14

    God recalls His care for Israel since Egypt, announces the fall of Samaria and ends with a call to repentance and a promise of healing.
