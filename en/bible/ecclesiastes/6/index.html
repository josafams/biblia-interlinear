Ecclesiastes 6

Goods that cannot be enjoyed

Open in the interlinear reader

6:1

יֵשׁthere רָעָהevil אֲשֶׁרhave רָאִיתִיseen תַּחַתunder הַשָּׁמֶשׁthe sun וְרַבָּהand weighs heavily הִיאis עַלupon הָאָדָםmankind

There is another evil I have seen under the sun, and it weighs heavily upon mankind:

6:2

אִישׁman אֲשֶׁרthat יִתֶּןgives לוֹa man הָאֱלֹהִיםGod עֹשֶׁרriches וּנְכָסִיםwealth וְכָבוֹדand honor וְאֵינֶנּוּso that he nothing חָסֵרlacks לְנַפְשׁוֹhis heart מִכֹּלfrom all אֲשֶׁרthat יִתְאַוֶּהdesires וְלֹאnot יַשְׁלִיטֶנּוּallow him הָאֱלֹהִיםGod לֶאֱכֹלto enjoy מִמֶּנּוּfrom us כִּיfor אִישׁman נָכְרִיstranger יֹאכֲלֶנּוּenjoy them זֶהthis הֶבֶלfutile וָחֳלִיand grievous רָעaffliction הוּאa

God gives a man riches, wealth, and honor, so that he lacks nothing his heart desires; but God does not allow him to enjoy them. Instead, a stranger will enjoy them. This is futile and a grievous affliction.

6:3

אִםif יוֹלִידfather אִישׁman מֵאָהhundred וְשָׁנִיםand years רַבּוֹתmany יִחְיֶהlive וְרַבfor many שֶׁיִּהְיוּmatter יְמֵיlong שָׁנָיוyears וְנַפְשׁוֹlives if he לֹאno תִשְׂבַּעunsatisfied מִןfrom הַטּוֹבָהhis prosperity וְגַםeven קְבוּרָהburial לֹאnot הָיְתָהwas לּוֹto him אָמַרְתִּיsay טוֹבbetter מִמֶּנּוּthan he הַנָּפֶלthat a stillborn child

A man may father a hundred children and live for many years; yet no matter how long he lives, if he is unsatisfied with his prosperity and does not even receive a proper burial, I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.

6:4

כִּיfor בַהֶבֶלa stillborn child בָּאenters וּבַחֹשֶׁךְand departs in darkness יֵלֵךְgo וּבַחֹשֶׁךְshrouded שְׁמוֹhis name יְכֻסֶּהobscurity

For a stillborn child enters in futility and departs in darkness, and his name is shrouded in obscurity.

6:5

גַּםeven שֶׁמֶשׁsun לֹאnot רָאָהseeing וְלֹאand not יָדָעknowing נַחַתanything לָזֶהto this מִזֶּהfrom this

The child, though neither seeing the sun nor knowing anything, has more rest than that man,

6:6

וְאִלּוּand if חָיָהlives אֶלֶףthousand שָׁנִיםyears פַּעֲמַיִםtwice וְטוֹבָהprosperity לֹאnot רָאָהenjoy הֲלֹאdo not אֶלto מָקוֹםplace אֶחָדsame הַכֹּלall הוֹלֵךְgo

even if he lives a thousand years twice over but fails to enjoy his prosperity. Do not all go to the same place?

6:7

כָּלall עֲמַלlabor הָאָדָםa man’s לְפִיהוּfor his mouth וְגַםand also הַנֶּפֶשׁhis appetite לֹאnever תִמָּלֵאsatisfied

All a man’s labor is for his mouth, yet his appetite is never satisfied.

6:8

כִּיfor מַהwhat יּוֹתֵרadvantage לֶחָכָםhas the wise מִןfrom הַכְּסִילthe fool מַהwhat לֶּעָנִיto the poor יוֹדֵעַknows לַהֲלֹךְto conduct נֶגֶדbefore הַחַיִּיםthe living

What advantage, then, has the wise man over the fool? What gain comes to the poor man who knows how to conduct himself before others?

6:9

טוֹבbetter מַרְאֵהeye עֵינַיִםsee מֵהֲלָךְthan the wandering נָפֶשׁdesire גַּםtoo זֶהthis is הֶבֶלfutile וּרְעוּתand a pursuit רוּחַwind

Better what the eye can see than the wandering of desire. This too is futile and a pursuit of the wind.

6:10

מַהwhat שֶּׁהָיָהexists was כְּבָרexists נִקְרָאcalled שְׁמוֹnamed וְנוֹדָעand what happens אֲשֶׁרthat הוּאis אָדָםman וְלֹאand not יוּכַלcannot לָדִיןcontend עִםwith שֶׁתַּקִּיףone stronger מִמֶּנּוּthan he

Whatever exists was named long ago, and what happens to a man is foreknown; but he cannot contend with one stronger than he.

6:11

כִּיfor יֵשׁthere דְּבָרִיםwords הַרְבֵּהmore מַרְבִּיםmore הָבֶלfutility מַהhow יֹּתֵרprofit לָאָדָםthat anyone

For the more words, the more futility—and how does that profit anyone?

6:12

כִּיfor מִיwho יוֹדֵעַknows מַהwhat טּוֹבgood לָאָדָםfor a man בַּחַיִּיםin the life מִסְפַּרfew יְמֵיdays חַיֵּיlife הֶבְלוֹhis fleeting וְיַעֲשֵׂםhe passes כַּצֵּלlike a shadow אֲשֶׁרwho מִיcan יַגִּידtell לָאָדָםa man מַהwhat יִּהְיֶהcome אַחֲרָיוafter him תַּחַתunder הַשָּׁמֶשׁthe sun

For who knows what is good for a man during the few days in which he passes through his fleeting life like a shadow? Who can tell a man what will come after him under the sun?
