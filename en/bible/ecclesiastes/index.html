Ecclesiastes קֹהֶלֶת

Ecclesiastes records the reflection of a sage called Qohelet, the Teacher, who presents himself as “the son of David, king in Jerusalem” and investigates what profit human beings gain from all their labor “under the sun.” He puts to the test wisdom, pleasure, great works and wealth, observes injustice and death, which comes equally to the wise and the fool, and concludes again and again that everything is “futile.” At the same time, he recommends enjoying with gladness bread, wine, work and life with the one you love, as gifts of God. The book ends with an epilogue that sums up the teaching: fear God and keep His commandments (12:13).

Structure

  1. Opening ch. 1

    The title, the motto “everything is futile” and a poem on the endless cycle of nature and of the generations (1:1–11).

  2. The king's experience chs. 1–2

    In the role of a king, the Teacher puts to the test wisdom, pleasure, building and wealth, and runs up against death (1:12–2:26).

  3. Time, injustice and work chs. 3–6

    The time for everything, oppression, loneliness, vows, money and possessions that bring no satisfaction (3:1–6:9).

  4. What cannot be known chs. 6–11

    Counsels and reflections on the limits of wisdom: no one knows for sure what is good or what is to come, but life should be lived with joy (6:10–11:6).

  5. Youth, old age and conclusion chs. 11–12

    A poem about enjoying youth before old age and death, the return of the motto and the epilogue (11:7–12:14).
